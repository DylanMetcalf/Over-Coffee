The identity, refined.

Over Coffee is a daily moment: you sit down, get informed about what you care about, and get on with your day. The identity says exactly that — a conversational italic “over”, a heavy “coffee”, and a full stop that means the edition ends.

How we got here

Eight directions were drawn and compared before choosing. The ring-“C.” from the previous version was kept in the running and honestly judged: clean and friendly, but it looks like many other apps and says nothing about reading or mornings. Concepts that depicted coffee or a page literally (B, E) were memorable but read as café or stationery branding, and failed at 16 px.

Why the winner. The type-led identity is the only direction that carries all three ideas — conversation, ritual, a finished edition — without drawing a cup or a page. It feels editorial rather than SaaS, has a recognisable silhouette (an italic serif beside a heavy grotesque) and a mark small enough to be an icon. The page-shaped C was the strongest symbol-only idea (ownable) but read as a camera or wallet and fought the wordmark. It is parked, not discarded.

What would make it better. The letterforms are set from open-licence fonts (Instrument Serif, Bricolage Grotesque). Before launch, a type designer should customise the “c” and “ff” joins to make the wordmark fully proprietary, and the identity should be tested with real users on real devices.

Rules

Use it like this

  • Keep clear space equal to the height of the “o” on every side.
  • Minimum wordmark height 16 px digital, 6 mm print. Below that, use the symbol alone.
  • The dot is always the accent red (a warmer red-orange on dark). The app icon is a quiet warm-paper tile with an ink letter; no gradients.
  • Wordmark on white or espresso black only. Never on photography without a scrim.

Never

  • Re-set the wordmark in another font or change the italic/bold pairing.
  • Drop the full stop, or add a second one.
  • Put the symbol in a circle, add a coffee cup, steam or a drop shadow.
  • Recolour “over” and “coffee” the same colour except in the monochrome version.
